Claudia, your home sat on the market for 84 days and didn't sell. That's a long time to wait — and a frustrating outcome for a property that genuinely has something to offer.

Here's what the data shows: the price was right. Five automated valuation sources all put the value between $288,500 and $295,000. The final ask of $295,000 was exactly where it needed to be. The market wasn't the problem. The presentation was.

**The Before/After**

What buyers saw in the listing: an empty great room under flat light, bare bedrooms with wire-shelf closets, a screened porch shot from inside looking out at a concrete slab. Twenty-seven photos of a vacant house that reads like a vacant house.

What buyers should have seen: a 575-square-foot great room anchored by a wood-burning fireplace, with coffered ceilings and enough natural light to stop a scroll. A screened porch that functions as a second living room. A home with a real story — updated, move-in ready, priced honestly.

Same home. Same market. The gap between those two things is where the listing fell short.

**Wow #1 — The Screened Porch**

Nearly 20 by 12 feet of covered outdoor living space — fully screened, with a beadboard ceiling and enough room for a dining table and a sitting area at the same time. For a buyer relocating from the Midwest or the Northeast, this isn't a nice-to-have. It's the reason they moved to Florida.

The MLS photo showed an empty concrete rectangle through jalousie glass. The room it actually is — the room where you spend every evening from October through May — never made it into the listing.

**Wow #2 — The Fireplace in a Room That Can Use It**

A wood-burning fireplace in a Florida home isn't unusual. A wood-burning fireplace in a 575-square-foot open great room with vaulted, coffered ceilings — that's a different thing entirely. The scale works. The fireplace becomes the center of the room, not a footnote in the corner of it.

Import buyers from colder climates respond to this in a way local buyers don't fully clock. It signals warmth, permanence, winter evenings worth staying home for. The listing copy mentioned it. The photos never let it land.

:::

**Wow #3 — The Renovation Arc**

New roof installed 2024. Fresh landscaping. Interior paint throughout. A home that has been genuinely maintained and recently improved — not just tidied up for photos.

The gap between what buyers saw in the listing photos and what the home actually is represents real, recoverable buyer interest. A buyer walking through this house in person responds differently than a buyer scrolling a set of vacant-room photos. The job of the listing is to close that gap before the showing. The prior listing didn't.

**What a BlueGecko Campaign Looks Like for This Home**

Every campaign starts with the Three Wows — the features that stop a buyer cold. For your home, those are the porch, the fireplace, and the renovation story. Every piece of marketing is built around surfacing those three things in a way that actually registers.

That means virtual staging to show the rooms as rooms, not as square footage. Listing copy written around what makes this home different, not a generic description of features. Photography direction that puts the screened porch in its best light — because that room sells the property.

The price was never the issue. The right buyer was out there during those 84 days. They just never got to see the home you actually have.
