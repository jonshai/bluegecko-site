**What the market has been saying**

Six independent automated valuation models place this home in a range of
$407,000 to $426,000. That range reflects the consensus of RPR, which draws
from MLS transaction data; Zillow and Redfin, which are the tools buyers use
before they ever contact an agent; and three independent models on Realtor.com.
All six land within $19,000 of each other. That is a tight consensus for this
price range — it means the market has a clear and consistent view of where
this home sits.

Your listing entered at $449,000 in late November 2025. The market was in a
briefly stable window — rate optimism had paused the decline and motivated buyers
were present. The entry price was above where every major valuation model placed
the home, which meant the buyers who were ready to move during that window passed
on a listing asking them to pay a premium the data did not support.

In mid-February, a major geopolitical event drove an unexpected inflation spike
and mortgage rates moved back up sharply. The market resumed its decline. From
that point forward, the target the listing was chasing was itself moving downward.
Seven reductions over 190 days closed part of the gap each time and the market
reopened it. The listing expired at $409,000 — inside the consensus range —
but by then the DOM number had become the story every buyer's agent was telling
their client before a showing was ever scheduled.

**Why the opening window matters**

A new listing gets one moment of peak visibility. The most motivated,
best-qualified buyers are watching the market continuously and they respond to
new listings immediately. Those buyers are not waiting for month four. They have
already bought something else.

There is a second mechanic worth understanding. Most search platforms let buyers
save favorites and hide listings they are not interested in. A listing priced too
high gets hidden — the buyer swipes left, so to speak, and moves on. Once a listing
is hidden, future price drops are invisible to that buyer because the listing no
longer appears in their feed at all. The buyer who would have been the right fit
at $419,000 never sees the listing get there, because they opted out at $449,000.
Getting the price right while those buyers are still actively watching is the only
way to reach them before they disappear from the audience entirely.

Pricing right from the start is not a concession. It is the strategy that captures
the best buyers during the best window — before the DOM counter starts doing the
talking instead of the home.

**What the comparable sales tell us**

Two recent sales in the Sebastian market illustrate what entry pricing does to
outcomes — not just whether a home sells, but how long it takes and what it costs
to get there.

1043 Landsdowne Drive closed at $366,000 in May 2026 — three days on market,
97.6% of the original ask. The sellers priced it correctly, the right buyers found
it immediately, and the process was over before the carry cost meter had time to
run. That is what a well-positioned entry produces.

625 Gossamer Wing Way tells a different story. Listed well above where current
valuations suggest the market would support — closer to $519,000 was the defensible
entry — it sat for 93 days and closed at $500,000 after two price reductions and
negotiation. The sellers still got a strong number, but the path to get there cost
them 90 additional days of carrying costs, two reductions, and the negotiating
leverage that a fresh listing commands and a stale one does not. Both homes sold
for what the market would bear. The difference is entirely in the experience of
getting there.

For 852 Jordan Ave, a well-supported entry in the $415,000–$420,000 range puts
this home at the top of the right search tier on day one, priced where the data
supports it and the right buyers are already looking.

**The two-week read**

The opening two weeks of any listing are the market's most honest feedback. Showing
volume, agent comments, and offer activity in that window tell you more about how
the market sees the price than six months of reductions ever will. We evaluate
that data together at the two-week mark and decide what it is telling us. Strong
showings with good feedback may mean we hold steady and work the agents who have
already been through the door. Light showing activity is the market telling us
something needs to change, and we move accordingly. The goal is to sell the house —
not to conduct free tours for the neighborhood while the carry cost meter runs.
