## How buyers actually search -- and why entry point matters

Buyers set price ceilings, not floors. A buyer searching homes up to $550,000 never sees a home listed at $551,000. They don't know it exists.

This sounds obvious. It matters enormously in practice.

A home priced at the top of a search bracket -- say, $549,000 -- reaches every buyer searching up to $550,000. That's a large pool. A home priced at $552,000 misses that entire pool and competes only with homes in the $550,000--$600,000 bracket, where it's likely the smallest and least-featured option.

There's also the days-on-market question. Buyers see DOM. A listing with 90 days on market carries a question mark that a fresh listing doesn't. Buyers assume that if a home has been sitting, something is wrong -- even when nothing is. A re-listing at the right price resets that clock and re-enters the new-listing surge, when buyer interest and showing volume are highest.

The bracket strategy: launch at the top of the right search tier, hold for 14 days, watch traffic. If buyers come but don't offer, move to the adjustment number and let the haggler feel like they won. That's not conceding -- that's executing.

## The presentation gap -- and what it means for you

The prior listing had professional photography. But over-sharpening in the processing created the appearance of surface issues in the bathroom and shower enclosure that a walkthrough would immediately dispel. Online, those photos raised a question. In person, there is no question.

There was no Matterport. No 3D Zillow tour. No floor plans.

For a buyer relocating from out of state -- which describes a meaningful share of this submarket's buyer pool -- the 3D tour is the showing. It's how they narrow from fifteen homes to five before they book a flight. A home without a 3D tour doesn't make that short list.

This isn't a criticism of what came before. It's a description of what a re-listing needs to include to reach the buyer who is already out there looking for exactly what this home offers.

New photography. Matterport. Drone shots that tell the location story. The home seen the way it actually looks.
